# Creative and visual style guide

## Creative voice

Prestige character drama with dark legal-comedy energy, but **not** a lecture series. Each episode uses one accessible moral dispute to reveal philosophical disagreement and human consequences. Humor comes from Sophie's advertising, verbal nimbleness, entrepreneurial incentives, and institutional absurdity — not from falsely depicting real philosophy as universally meaningless.

## Dialogue

- Sophie's arguments should sound precise, playful and commercially adept. She frequently separates "illegal," "immoral," "unpopular," and "unjustified."
- Every important opponent gets a serious intellectual defense, not a straw man.
- Explain philosophical concepts through decisions, concessions, confrontations and stakes, not uninterrupted exposition.
- Show what a position entails, not merely which philosopher's name can be dropped.
- Maintain legal realism: philosophical advocacy does not grant courtroom representation or attorney-client privilege.
- Don't equate logical validity with empirical truth; actively distinguish semantics when drama depends on them.

## Visual brand (current concept, not locked casting)

- Goodman advertising: punchy **yellow, red, black**, bold salesmanship, oversized typography, playful slogan placement.
- Drama photography: cinematic contrast, Albuquerque sunset, grounded architecture, office clutter, books and diagrams.
- Sophie concept: female lead, early 30s, dark wavy hair tied loosely, sharp burgundy suit over ivory blouse, gold jewelry, wry confident smile.
- Props: annotated philosophical texts, whiteboards with moral frameworks, odd educational keepsakes, Saul-like business cards.
- Keep corporate and university settings grounded and varied rather than generic futuristic glass rooms.
- Avoid actual copyrighted footage, actors' likenesses or franchise logo reproduction unless rights are separately reviewed.

## Production continuity

For each shot identify: scene, emotional purpose, location, time of day, participants, exact appearance/wardrobe, props, camera scale/motion, lighting, and previous/next shot. Carry stable reference images and a character sheet through all generations. Inspect hands, faces, text, eyelines, lip sync, background changes, and spatial continuity.

## Screen formats

Default planning assumption: **16:9 horizontal video**. The existing poster is **4:3** and the card front/back are landscape marketing assets; they are not episode frame specifications. Confirm final master formats before rendering. Design title-safe zones and captions without cropping essential elements.

## Sound

Voices are fictional and generated; document allowed models and voice identities. Use only licensed or originally generated score/SFX, with continuity on room tone and speaking pace. Provide human-readable transcripts/subtitles and dialogue checks.
