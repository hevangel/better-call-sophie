# S01E01 — The Goodman Defense

**Status:** PROPOSED TREATMENT — no completed script, generated clip or final scene list.

## Logline

After a company executive is condemned for replacing workers with AI, Sophie Goodman argues that defending obsolete work may itself impose moral costs — but learns that her client's motives and treatment of employees are more complicated than his public statement.

## Story engine

- **Client demand:** Recover public trust and permit a controversial restructuring.
- **Opposition:** Displaced employees and philosopher-led critics; they make credible claims about promises, dignity, concentrated harm and the transition burden.
- **Sophie's first case:** Compare aggregate welfare gains with losses, including the possibility that blocking beneficial technology also harms outsiders.
- **Counterattack:** Good ends do not absolve exploitative process or broken obligations.
- **Metaethical escalation:** What grounds the institution's normative priority for job preservation rather than future welfare?
- **Cost:** A polished defense may give Sophie a large fee but leave her morally implicated in the company's actual decisions.

## Sample scene (illustrative, not screenplay canon)

CLIENT: "Can you prove I'm a good person?"
SOPHIE: "No. But I can make your critics regret calling you a bad one."
